# Technology roadmap

<p class="page-lead">The public-facing roadmap shows how SMILE is expected to progress from targets and benchmarks to candidate designs, prototypes, and bounded demonstrations between 2026 and 2029.</p>

<div class="callout">
  <strong>How to read this roadmap.</strong> It is a communication view of the research logic, not a reproduction of contractual work packages. Activities overlap, and the emphasis may evolve as modelling, experiments, and participant studies generate evidence.
</div>

## 2026 · Foundations

<div class="roadmap-card">
  <span class="card-tag">Observe and define</span>
  <h3>Establish reference scenarios, targets, and benchmarks</h3>
  <p>Combine acoustic measurements, contextual information, and participant-reported soundscape outcomes to define the questions that an engineered intervention should answer. Establish modelling, measurement, and evaluation protocols suitable for later comparison.</p>
</div>

**Expected evidence:** reference scenarios; explicit physical and perceptual targets; benchmark models and measurements; documented uncertainty and practical constraints.

## 2027 · Design

<div class="roadmap-card">
  <span class="card-tag">Model and inverse-design</span>
  <h3>Search for feasible candidate structures</h3>
  <p>Use physics-based forward models, experimental characterisation, and machine-learning-assisted optimisation to explore a high-dimensional design space. Rank candidates across acoustic performance, robustness, space, mass, manufacturability, and sustainability rather than optimising a single number.</p>
</div>

**Expected evidence:** verified forward-model behaviour; transparent objective functions; sensitivity analysis; ranked candidates whose constraints are traceable.

## 2028 · Prototype

<div class="roadmap-card">
  <span class="card-tag">Build and test</span>
  <h3>Compare selected concepts with predictions and baselines</h3>
  <p>Manufacture selected concepts, measure their acoustic response, and compare the results with simulations and baseline conditions. Disagreement is used to revise models, assumptions, or the design range—not hidden behind a single performance score.</p>
</div>

**Expected evidence:** documented prototypes; repeatable measurements; prediction-to-test comparisons; revised models and uncertainty bounds.

## 2029 · Demonstrate

<div class="roadmap-card">
  <span class="card-tag">Evaluate and translate</span>
  <h3>Test supported application scenarios and communicate the limits</h3>
  <p>Evaluate whether physically verified interventions affect participant-reported outcomes in selected contexts. Translate supported findings into demonstrators, publications, design guidance, and reusable outputs where ethics, licensing, and collaboration agreements permit.</p>
</div>

**Expected evidence:** application-specific evaluation; supported design range; evidence-backed guidance; clear separation between tested cases and future opportunities.

## Decision gates

Progression is based on evidence rather than calendar dates alone.

<div class="evidence-grid">
  <div class="evidence-item physical">
    <strong>Physical gate</strong>
    Is the predicted acoustic effect observed, repeatable, and meaningfully different from the baseline within the stated uncertainty?
  </div>
  <div class="evidence-item human">
    <strong>Perceptual gate</strong>
    Does a participant study support the hypothesised outcome for the tested context and protocol?
  </div>
  <div class="evidence-item practical">
    <strong>Translation gate</strong>
    Is the design feasible under relevant space, mass, robustness, manufacturing, and sustainability constraints?
  </div>
</div>

## Continuous research streams

Five streams connect the four phases:

1. **Soundscape and context** — define the experience and use case that motivate the target.
2. **Physics and modelling** — predict the sound field and make uncertainty visible.
3. **Metamaterial design** — connect geometry and material choices to acoustic behaviour.
4. **Learning and optimisation** — accelerate constrained search without replacing physical verification.
5. **Evaluation and translation** — test prototypes, participant-reported outcomes, and application limits.
