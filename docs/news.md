## Project news

### Autumn 2026 — An incoming postdoctoral researcher

SMILE is preparing to welcome a postdoctoral researcher to KTH. The new colleague will strengthen the project’s capacity in computational acoustics, acoustic metamaterials, physical evaluation, and human-centred soundscape research. The researcher will be introduced when the appointment details are public. [Read the original KTH role description](https://www.kth.se/lediga-jobb/954277?l=en).

### 1 June 2026 — KTH–UCL workshop and visit

A University College London team visited KTH for a joint workshop on soundscape design and acoustic metamaterials. The afternoon at KTH’s Teknikringen campus combined a guided soundwalk with discussion of how metamaterials might contribute to soundscape quality in vehicle and urban settings. Xiang Fang and Tin Oberman led the introductory sessions, followed by a collaborative workshop and discussion. The visit created a shared starting point for future work across the two research environments.

### 2025–2026 — SMILE City: A Pre-study

**SMILE City** brings KTH and UCL researchers together to explore how soundscape thinking and acoustic metamaterials may inform responses to traffic noise in urban public space. The pre-study was selected for Stockholm Trio–UCL seed funding, supporting early exchange and longer-term collaboration. [Read the UCL announcement](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/global/news/2025/jul/ucl-and-stockholm-trio-award-seed-funding-four-new-collaborative-research-projects) or [the Stockholm Trio announcement](https://www.stockholmtrio.org/news/news/fourprojectsreceiveseedfundingforcollaborationbetweenstockholmtrioanduniversitycollegelondonucl.5.3104e4ad197a2309b5a7ea.html).

### Influence of traffic speed and acoustic environment on perception and perceived workload in virtual reality

<p class="research-meta">6 October 2026 · Peer reviewed · Perception and evaluation</p>

### Restorative urban soundscapes

<p class="research-meta">5 November 2026 · Peer reviewed · Soundscape design</p>

This review links acoustic scenes and perceptual processing to physiological, cognitive, and subjective restoration. It argues that restorative effects depend on perceived meaning, audiovisual congruence, listener and cultural differences, and measurement choices, and calls for conditional, testable design recommendations rather than generic prescriptions to add natural sounds.

### Absorbing exceptional point of coherent vortex beams

<p class="research-meta">15 October 2026 · Peer reviewed · Acoustic metamaterials</p>

Experiments with acoustic vortex beams and symmetry-tailored metasurfaces realised chiral coherent-perfect-absorption exceptional points. Rotating the metasurface switched absorption capacity and degenerate states, while the vortex beams’ orbital-angular-momentum channels increased channel capacity and enabled sensitivity alongside robust perfect absorption.

### Single-particle limit of a topological edge state in a locally resonant band gap

<p class="research-meta">5 November 2026 · Peer reviewed · Topological metamaterials</p>

A theoretical and numerical study adds a local resonator to an SSH-inspired stiffness dimer. Matching a topological edge state to the resonator-induced attenuation singularity confines the mode to one boundary particle at low frequency; boundary tuning can recover this idealised state after disorder detunes it.
