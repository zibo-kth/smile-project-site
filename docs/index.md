<span class="section-kicker">The research question</span>

## How can perception inform acoustic engineering?

Sound level alone does not describe how an acoustic environment is perceived in context. At the same time, a desirable soundscape cannot be engineered without measurable physical targets, practical constraints, and evidence that an intervention performs as intended.

SMILE studies the bridge between these layers: **what people report**, **what an acoustic structure changes**, and **what can be built and tested reliably**.

## Design, test, and learn

<div class="science-figure scrollable" tabindex="0" aria-label="Scrollable diagram of the five-stage SMILE research workflow">
  <img src="assets/smile-system-map.svg" alt="Five-stage SMILE research workflow from observing soundscapes to modelling, inverse design, prototyping, participant evaluation, and evidence-led refinement">
</div>

The workflow separates three questions that are sometimes conflated: **Does the device change the sound field?** **Do people report the intended effect in the tested context?** **Is the solution practical within the defined constraints?** A supported design decision needs evidence at all three levels.

<div class="callout">
  <strong>Project scope.</strong> SMILE develops and evaluates a perception-informed engineering workflow. It does not assume that a physically effective device will automatically improve experience; that relationship must be tested with participant-based studies.
</div>

  </div>
  <div class="info-card">
    <span class="card-tag">Outdoor scenario</span>
    <h3>Traffic-exposed public space</h3>

  </div>

These are **concept scenarios**, not claims of deployed interventions. The project roadmap moves from targets and benchmarks to candidate designs, prototypes, and bounded demonstrations.

## SMILE at a glance

The official Swedish title is **SMILE: Ljudlandskapsutformning med Metamaterialinnovation och Lärande**. The public-facing English expansion is **Soundscape engineering with Metamaterial Innovation and LEarning**.

SMILE is funded by the **Swedish Research Council**, led by **Zibo Liu**, and administered by **KTH Royal Institute of Technology, Department of Engineering Mechanics**. Research collaboration connects expertise at KTH, University College London, and Scania.
